# Volume 3. Biographical Case Studies

## Why this Volume

Rules without examples quickly become dead. Human stories of choice, price, and responsibility are needed.
Biographical case studies in the Code are not icons for worship, but working models: how a specific person in specific circumstances made a choice in favor of constructive action.

## Format of an Article

Each biographical case study is built according to a single scheme:

1. **Brief biography** — who this person is, in what context they acted.
2. **Two key episodes** — specific situations of choice where the principles of the Code manifested in action.
3. **3–5 applicable lessons** — what from this experience can be transferred to one's own life.
4. **Practice of memory** — a specific action that helps to retain the lesson.

## Blocks of Biographical Case Studies

### Pioneers

People who opened new paths — in science, technology, social life — and at the same time maintained responsibility to those who will come after.
*Example type:* a scientist who published data openly, knowing that it would slow down his career but accelerate overall progress.

### Guardians of Order

People who held institutions, rules, and agreements at a time when it was more profitable to break them.
*Example type:* a judge, official, or military officer who refused to carry out an illegal order and accepted the price for it.

### Ascetics of Progress

People who consciously chose sufficiency — giving up the excessive for the sake of the quality of work and life.
*Example type:* an entrepreneur or scientist who could have earned more but chose a smaller scale for the sake of greater honesty.

### Researchers of Harmony

People who sought a balance between contradictory values — and found it not in compromise, but in a deeper understanding.
*Example type:* a diplomat or public figure who found a solution where others saw only conflict.

### Martyrs of Errors

People who publicly admitted their mistakes, corrected them, and continued their work — without breaking down or going into denial.
*Example type:* a scientist or journalist who published a refutation of their own work when new data appeared.

### Conductors of Consciousness

Media creators, writers, artists who used their influence to increase the quality of thinking, rather than for manipulation.
*Example type:* an author who refused a lucrative contract because the material would have been harmful to the audience.

### Sowers (Educators and Teachers)

Teachers, mentors, parents who passed on not only knowledge, but also a culture of honest thinking and responsible action.
*Example type:* an educator who created an environment where a mistake is part of learning, not a reason for shame.

### Public Witnesses (Journalists)

Journalists and reporters who documented reality honestly — even when it was dangerous or unprofitable.
*Example type:* a reporter who gave up a sensation for the sake of accuracy and lost in reach, but maintained the audience's trust.

## Principle of Selecting Case Studies

A biographical case study is included in the Code if:

- the person's choice was real (there was an alternative, there was a price);
- the example is applicable to modern situations;
- the story does not idealize the person, but shows a specific choice in specific circumstances.

Examples from different cultures, eras, and professions are intentional: constructive action does not have one nationality or one profession.
