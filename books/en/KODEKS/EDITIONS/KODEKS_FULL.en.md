# Codex of Constructive Society

## Preamble

We know how to build rockets, edit genomes, and process billions of data points per second.
But the question of "how to live together without destroying what we have created" still remains without a clear answer.

This document is for those who make decisions: in the family, in the editorial office, in business, in politics.
It doesn't matter how large the scale is. What matters is that decisions have consequences for other people.

The Code does not claim to be a new ideology or religion.
It offers a working ethical standard — a set of verifiable principles that help:

- distinguish constructive action from destructive action,
- rely on truth and verifiable facts,
- make decisions with human dignity in mind,
- connect personal responsibility with the common good,
- maintain a long horizon of development, rather than just immediate profit.

The Code is compatible with laws, religions, cultures, and traditions.
It does not cancel them — it sets a common language for talking about consequences.

## II. Man and Everyday Responsibility

### 1) Close Relationships as Labor

Constructive action begins not only with large projects, but also with the quality of relationships with loved ones:

- respect,
- dialogue,
- help,
- responsibility for one's actions.

Relationships with loved ones are not a "private trifle," but a basic form of constructive action: through them, a person learns truth, responsibility, and respect for another's vulnerability.

What this means in practice:

- regular live communication, not formal contact;
- practical help, especially when a person is in difficulty;
- honest conversation about pain and boundaries without humiliation;
- readiness to change one's behavior, rather than just demanding changes from another.

Conflict does not always mean "everything is broken." Often it is a signal for reassembly: to clarify expectations, to apologize, to make a new agreement.
If a relationship consistently reproduces lies or the destruction of personality, an honest separation can be more creative than prolonged destruction.

Foundation:
`books/en/CODEX_CHELOVEKA/08_blizkie_otnosheniya_i_osoznannyy_vybor.en.md`

### 2) Regular Practices

Values are stable only when they translate into repeatable actions.
Meaning is maintained by repetition — not through mysticism, but through action that changes the quality of life.

Examples of practices:

- **Morning rule** — a short check: what is important today and why.
- **Fact-checking** — do not spread unverified information, clarify the source before sharing.
- **Reviewing mistakes without shame** — analyzing what went wrong, without self-flagellation and without shifting blame.
- **Useful team actions** — contribution to the common cause, not just personal efficiency.
- **Practice of sufficiency** — a conscious choice not to take more than is needed for the task.
- **Practice of memory and continuity** — passing on experience, not just accumulating it.

A practice is considered good if, after it, order, respect for people, and clarity of thought have increased in life.

Foundation:
`books/en/CODEX_CHELOVEKA/04_ritualy_i_povsednevnye_praktiki.en.md`

## III. Society, Institutions, Public Life

### 1) Politics and Defense: Ethics of the Agreement

Strength does not equal escalation.
Mature strength prevents destruction, maintains the dignity of the parties, and preserves the future after the conflict.

Love for the country is expressed in the quality of institutions, care for people, and respect for the truth — and not in the cult of enmity towards other nations.
A person can be deeply nationally rooted and at the same time understand the common destiny of the planet. This is not a contradiction, but a sign of maturity.

### 3) Media Creators and Educators: Conductors of Meaning

Media creators and educators shape how people understand the world, make decisions, and relate to each other. This makes their work socially significant — regardless of the size of the audience.

Media creator in the logic of the Code:

- does not produce content for the sake of engagement at any cost;
- checks facts and indicates sources;
- distinguishes between opinion and fact;
- does not use emotional manipulation as a primary tool.

Educator in the logic of the Code:

- teaches how to think, not just how to memorize;
- creates an environment where a mistake is part of learning, not a reason for humiliation;
- passes on not only knowledge, but also a culture of checking and updating knowledge;
- respects the dignity of each student regardless of their success.

Any new technology is evaluated through dignity, fairness of access, transparency, and public benefit.

Foundation:
`books/en/OBSHCHEE_BLAGO_I_SPRAVEDLIVAYA_EKONOMIKA/08_eticheskiy_test_tekhnologii_7_voprosov.en.md`

### 5) Bioethics: A Person Cannot Be Raw Material

Technically possible is not always ethically permissible.
Biotechnologies — cloning, genome editing, therapeutic interventions — are evaluated by the same criterion as any other technology: do they reduce suffering and expand a dignified life without turning a person into a commodity or expendable material.

Red lines:

- reproductive cloning as an industry of "custom-made people";
- selection of people based on profit or market demand;
- any biotechnological practices without voluntary informed consent.

Bioprogress is justified where it helps people live longer, healthier, and freer lives without destroying the moral framework of society.

Foundation:
`books/en/OBSHCHEE_BLAGO_I_SPRAVEDLIVAYA_EKONOMIKA/07_etika_klonirovaniya_i_dostoinstvo_cheloveka.en.md`

## VI. Earth and Space: A Common Horizon

Why is this topic in the Code? Because the choice between "protecting the Earth" and "going into space" is a false dilemma.
A civilization that does not care for its foundation will not survive long enough to go beyond it. A civilization that does not look beyond the Earth risks concentrating all vulnerabilities in one point.

Earth and space do not compete.
Earth is the foundation of life, culture, and responsibility.
Space is the horizon for the sustainable expansion of opportunities and the long-term security of humanity.

Any space project must bring measurable benefit to life on Earth and not worsen the basic conditions for people "here and now."
Any earthly project must strengthen humanity's capacity for exploration and constructive action, rather than closing this horizon.

## VII. Working with Knowledge and Updates

Transparency of sources is not an academic formality. It is an ethical norm.

When a person or institution hides what their statement is based on, they deprive others of the opportunity to check and challenge it. This is a form of power over someone else's thinking. The Code considers such power unacceptable.

Therefore, knowledge in the logic of the Code rests on three requirements:

- **the source is transparent** — it is clear where the statement is taken from and who stands behind it;
- **the conclusion is reproducible** — another person with the same data can arrive at the same result or honestly challenge it;
- **outdated data is updated publicly** — revising a position in the light of new facts is not a weakness, but a sign of maturity.

This applies to journalism, science, politics, business, and personal judgments equally.

## VIII. Social Contract of the Code

The Code is not a declaration of intent. It is an agreement on how to test decisions in practice.

The agreement works on three levels:

**Personal level.**
A person takes responsibility for the consequences of their words and actions — in the family, in the profession, in the public space.

**Social level.**
Institutions — media, schools, courts, business — are evaluated not by declarations, but by whether they strengthen trust and the quality of people's lives.

**Civilizational level.**
Technologies, economic models, and political decisions are tested on a long horizon: what will remain in 20–50 years?

### Performance Criterion

The Code is considered working if, after its application in a specific situation:

- it has become clearer who the decision really helps and who it harms;
- the probability of long-term destruction has decreased;
- trust between participants has grown;
- the decision can be openly explained to any person it affects.

In a broader horizon, the Code works if the following grow in society:

- quality of life,
- level of trust,
- society's ability to learn,
- sustainability of decisions in the long horizon.

### Short Formula

Truth instead of a convenient lie.
Constructive action instead of destructive action.
Dignity instead of dehumanization.
Common good instead of narrow profit.

## Appendix: What Lives Nearby and Why

Two books complement the Code as a contextual layer — they expand understanding, but do not replace the accepted layer of norms.

**`books/en/TEKHNOKOSMOS_I_FRONTIR/INDEX.en.md`** — scenarios and contours of development.
Here: why expansion is needed, what professions the frontier forms, how to combine risk and responsibility. This is not science fiction, but practical ethics of a long horizon.

**`books/en/SVETSKAYA_RELIGIYA_SMYSLA/INDEX.en.md`** — social forms, practices, risks of distortion.
Here: how to create spaces of meaning without coercion, how the hierarchy by contribution is structured, what risks arise when institutionalizing ethics. This book does not repeat the Code — it explores what happens when the values of the Code are attempted to be embodied in living communities and institutions.

The difference between the Code and these books:
The Code is the normative layer (what is considered right and why).
Contextual books are the analytical layer (how it works in reality, what scenarios are possible, where distortions arise).
